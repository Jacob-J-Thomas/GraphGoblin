# #40 font choices: the shortlist in the app, for the owner to prune

Date: 2026-10-06. Built from commit `0fd26cf` with `pnpm.cmd build`; full matrix captured 2026-10-06. The real app, served by `apps/web/e2e/server.ts` (the in-memory API with the fake harness), with seeded loops, runs, and inbound events. Edge (Chromium), reduced motion, viewport screenshots taken after the page fonts finished loading. Each face is chosen the way Settings → Appearance → Font does it (the stored `graphgoblin-font`, which the boot script in `index.html` applies before first paint), in Dark and Light.

**The owner's checkpoint:** choose which of these faces ship. All six are selectable in this build so they can be compared in place; the issue stays open until the shortlist is pruned. Removing a face is a small change (its `@font-face` and choice rule in `styles/fonts.css`, its id in `src/lib/font.ts`, its files and licence in `apps/web/public/fonts`, and its row in `docs/research/licenses.md`).

Regenerate with `node docs/qa/2026-10-05-issue-40-fonts/capture.mjs` after `pnpm.cmd build`, then `pnpm.cmd exec prettier --write docs/qa/2026-10-05-issue-40-fonts` for `metrics.json`. The ten viewport screens are Loops, Runs, Events, New run, Not found, the editor with its Decision dialog, the editor with its inference dialog, the run inspector, the first Settings viewport, and full-page Settings. Each is captured at 1024x768 and 1440x900 in Dark and Light for all six faces. Those same ten captures are repeated at 200% in both themes, and the bare editor canvas is also captured at 200% in both themes. The Font control previews are captured in both themes. The app fixture uses its fixed fake clock so seeded timestamps and event data are stable. `GG_CAPTURE_ONLY=geist,inter` takes only some faces and `GG_CAPTURE_SCREENS=settings,settings-full,font-control` only some supported captures; a partial run replaces only its own entries in `metrics.json`. After the shortlist is pruned, delete removed faces folders; their metrics are dropped on the next run.

## What the measurements say

- **The intended face is drawn in all 372 metric captures.** The browser drew text and headings in the expected families (Chakra Petch for headings and Geist for text), and code, ids, and timeline event types in Geist Mono. Space Grotesk reports its family as "Space Grotesk Light", the name of its variable file's default instance.
- **No horizontal page scroll** in any face, theme, or size, including 200% zoom.
- **Inner horizontal scroll:** only OpenDyslexic, on the Events table inside its card at 1024 px in both themes. At 200% none of the faces has inner horizontal scrolling; the Events table still fits. It does not make the page itself wider.
- **Cut text:** none in Geist, Space Grotesk, Chakra Petch, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, or Inter. In OpenDyslexic, three node card labels ("Triage new issues", "Record outcome", "Approve merge") end in an ellipsis in both editor-dialog captures at both standard widths and themes, and in both dialogs and the bare editor canvas at 200% in both themes. The full labels remain in the corresponding dialog fields.
- **Settings:** the full-page captures show no page overflow or clipped text for any face.

## Readability notes per face

- **Geist.** The baseline: compact, even, and neutral. Nothing cut or wrapped beyond today's layout.
- **Space Grotesk.** Idiosyncratic letters from its Space Mono origins (the "y", "G", and "a" especially) give the app a technical, slightly mischievous voice without hurting dense text: the decision dialog's labels, the palette's two-line descriptions, and the timeline read as easily as in Geist. Like Geist, its "I" and "l" are the same stroke, and "O" and "0" differ only in width. About 8% wider than Geist, and none of the captured text was cut.
- **Chakra Petch (headings and wordmark).** The most "robotic goblin" look for the least risk: page titles, card and dialog headings, the loop name in the editor toolbar, and the wordmark take the squared, chamfered letters; field labels, tables, node cards, and the timeline stay in Geist in the captured screens. Parentheses become square-ish, which reads well in "Timeline (16 events, live)". As a text face it would be harder going (my judgement from the specimen): its squared "O" and "0" are nearly the same shape, and long runs of its angular lowercase tire the eye at 13 px, which is why it is offered for headings only.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.** The clearest text at small sizes: "I" has serifs, "l" a tail, "1" a flag, and "0" a slash, so ids and labels in the dialog and settings are hard to misread. Slightly narrower than Geist, so every captured screen keeps its layout; the timeline and node cards look the same size as in Geist. Its subset has no "→" (the font has none), which the browser draws from the system font in the timeline ("start → out").
- **OpenDyslexic.** Heavy bottoms and very wide spacing: one line of 13 px text is 77% wider than in Geist (401 px against 227 px in the specimen), and every word is letter-spaced. In the captures, three node card labels end in an ellipsis behind both editor dialogs at standard widths and themes, and in both dialogs and the bare editor canvas at 200% in both themes. At 1024 px, Loops names and descriptions wrap into short lines, and the Events table scrolls horizontally inside its card in both themes. The full Settings page has no page overflow or clipped text; the metrics do not report additional inner horizontal scrolling there. Timeline and palette descriptions wrap more than in Geist. Event types, ids, and JSON stay in Geist Mono, which looks small beside it. It has only Regular and Bold (500 shows Regular, 600 shows Bold). Its Reserved Font Name means it ships unmodified (Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, and more), hence 206 KiB.
- **Inter.** Neutral and very legible, with a slightly larger look than Geist at the same size (taller x-height) and 4% wider lines; no text is cut in the captured screenshots. Its glyphs and spacing are close to Geist's, so as a "different preference" it is the least different.

## How the shortlist was chosen

- **Display face.** The brief's candidates were Space Grotesk, Chakra Petch, Michroma, and Orbitron (`specimen-display-candidates.png`). Michroma has one weight and is far wider than Geist (the 13 px sample wraps to three lines where Geist takes two), so it would overflow dense screens and its bold would be synthesised. Orbitron is built from geometric, capital-like shapes: its lowercase and digits (a "0" slashed like "Ø") are hard to read at UI sizes, it lacks the "·" the app uses, and it carries a Reserved Font Name. Chakra Petch is the most robotic of the readable ones, at Geist's width; Space Grotesk is the most characterful that also works as a text face. Both are offered, in the two shapes the issue described: one face throughout, or a separate display family for headings only.
- **Easier reading.** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (2025, OFL, variable 200 to 800) rather than the 2019 Atkinson Hyperlegible, which has only Regular and Bold. OpenDyslexic is the face the issue named and some readers look for by name; published studies have not found that it improves reading speed or accuracy, and its width is a real cost here, so the notes above give the owner both sides.
- **Neutral.** Inter rather than IBM Plex Sans: Plex's licence reserves the name "Plex", so a subset of our own could not keep the name, and shipping IBM's own files instead means one file per weight. Inter has no Reserved Font Name, is one variable file, and was designed for user interfaces at small sizes. The trade-off is that Inter is closer to Geist than Plex would be.
