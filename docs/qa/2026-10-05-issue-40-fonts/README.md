# #40 font choices: the shortlist in the app, for the owner to prune

Date: 2026-10-05. The real app, built with `pnpm build` and served by `apps/web/e2e/server.ts` (the in-memory API with the fake harness), with seeded loops and runs. Edge (Chromium), reduced motion, viewport screenshots taken after the page's fonts finished loading. Each face is chosen the way Settings → Appearance → Font does it (the stored `graphgoblin-font`, which the boot script in `index.html` applies before first paint), in Dark and Light.

**The owner's checkpoint:** choose which of these faces ship. All six are selectable in this build so they can be compared in place; the issue stays open until the shortlist is pruned. Removing a face is a small change (its `@font-face` and choice rule in `styles/fonts.css`, its id in `src/lib/font.ts`, its files and licence in `apps/web/public/fonts`, and its row in `docs/research/licenses.md`).

Regenerate with `node docs/qa/2026-10-05-issue-40-fonts/capture.mjs` after `pnpm build` (`GG_CAPTURE_ONLY=geist,inter` takes only some faces), then `pnpm exec prettier --write docs/qa/2026-10-05-issue-40-fonts` for `metrics.json`.

## Files

- `<face>/<screen>-<theme>-<size>.png`: every face in its folder (`geist`, `space-grotesk`, `chakra-petch`, `atkinson-hyperlegible`, `opendyslexic`, `inter`), each screen in `dark` and `light` at `1024` (1024x768) and `1440` (1440x900):
  - `loops`: the Loops list (create and import, five loops with publish state and last run).
  - `editor-dialog`: the editor on `nightly-triage` (one node of each kind and a loop-back) with the decision node's dialog open (routes, descriptions, the palette and canvas behind it).
  - `inspector`: the run inspector on a waiting run: the input form, the timeline (16 events, live), and the thread.
  - `settings`: the top of Settings, with the Appearance card and the Font control showing the face in use.
- `<face>/editor-dark-200pct.png`: the editor canvas at 200% zoom (a 1440x900 window at 200%: a 720x450 CSS viewport at device scale 2), so the node cards show at the size a zoomed reader sees them.
- `font-control-dark.png`, `font-control-light.png`: the Font control at device scale 2, keyboard focus on the chosen face. Each option names itself in its own heading face, with its description in its own text face.
- `specimen-display-candidates.png`: the four display candidates the brief named (Space Grotesk, Chakra Petch, Michroma, Orbitron) against Geist, as the wordmark, a page title, a card heading, a 150 px node card label, and 13 px text. Drawn from the upstream files in a scratch page, not the app.
- `specimen-text-faces.png`: the text faces against Geist: look-alike letters at 20 px, a 13 px paragraph, 12 px labels, and the width of one 13 px line relative to Geist (subset files, scratch page).
- `metrics.json`: per screenshot, the face on `<html>`, horizontal page scroll, text cut off by an ellipsis or a clip, and the fonts the browser actually drew the first text run and the first heading in (`CSS.getPlatformFontsForNode`).

## What the measurements say

- **The intended face is drawn everywhere.** In all 102 screenshots the browser drew text and headings in the chosen face (Chakra Petch: headings in Chakra Petch, text in Geist), and code, ids, and the timeline's event types in Geist Mono. Space Grotesk reports its family as "Space Grotesk Light", the name of its variable file's default instance.
- **No horizontal page scroll** in any face, theme, or size, including 200% zoom.
- **Cut text:** none in Geist, Space Grotesk, Chakra Petch, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, or Inter. In OpenDyslexic, three node card labels on the canvas end in an ellipsis ("Triage new issues", "Record outcome", "Approve merge") at every size; the full label stays in the node's name and its dialog.
- **The other screens, measured but not photographed** (the same checks in a scratch sweep, Dark, every face at 1024 and 1440 px, 180 page loads): Runs, Events, New run, Not found, the whole Settings page, the run inspector, and the dialog of every node kind (trigger, inference, decision, script, heartbeat, mutate, wait, exit). No page scroll and no cut text in any face, except in OpenDyslexic: the same three canvas labels behind each dialog, and at 1024 px the Events table scrolls sideways inside its card, as the Loops table does.

## Readability notes per face

- **Geist.** The baseline: compact, even, and neutral. Nothing cut or wrapped beyond today's layout.
- **Space Grotesk.** Idiosyncratic letters from its Space Mono origins (the "y", "G", and "a" especially) give the app a technical, slightly mischievous voice without hurting dense text: the decision dialog's labels, the palette's two-line descriptions, and the timeline read as easily as in Geist. Like Geist, its "I" and "l" are the same stroke, and "O" and "0" differ only in width. About 8% wider than Geist, which none of the screens needed to absorb by cutting text.
- **Chakra Petch (headings and wordmark).** The most "robotic goblin" look for the least risk: page titles, card and dialog headings, the loop name in the editor toolbar, and the wordmark take the squared, chamfered letters; field labels, tables, node cards, and the timeline stay in Geist, so no dense screen changes width. Parentheses become square-ish, which reads well in "Timeline (16 events, live)". As a text face it would be harder going (my judgement from the specimen): its squared "O" and "0" are nearly the same shape, and long runs of its angular lowercase tire the eye at 13 px, which is why it is offered for headings only.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.** The clearest text at small sizes: "I" has serifs, "l" a tail, "1" a flag, and "0" a slash, so ids and labels in the dialog and settings are hard to misread. Slightly narrower than Geist, so every screen keeps its layout; the timeline and node cards look the same size as in Geist. Its subset has no "→" (the font has none), which the browser draws from the system font in the timeline ("start → out").
- **OpenDyslexic.** Heavy bottoms and very wide spacing: one line of 13 px text is 77% wider than in Geist (401 px against 227 px in the specimen), and every word is letter-spaced. On dense screens that means: three node card labels cut with an ellipsis on the canvas; at 1024 px the Loops table's names and descriptions wrap a word per line and its Actions column scrolls sideways inside the card (at 1440 px it fits), and the timeline's longer descriptions wrap to two lines; the palette's descriptions take three lines. Nothing overlaps or leaves the page, and every action stays reachable. Event types, ids, and JSON stay in Geist Mono, which looks small beside it. It has only Regular and Bold (500 shows Regular, 600 shows Bold). Its Reserved Font Name means it ships unmodified (Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, and more), hence 206 KiB.
- **Inter.** Neutral and very legible, with a slightly larger look than Geist at the same size (taller x-height) and 4% wider lines; nothing is cut or wraps differently in any screenshot. Its glyphs and spacing are close to Geist's, so as a "different preference" it is the least different.

## How the shortlist was chosen

- **Display face.** The brief's candidates were Space Grotesk, Chakra Petch, Michroma, and Orbitron (`specimen-display-candidates.png`). Michroma has one weight and is far wider than Geist (the 13 px sample wraps to three lines where Geist takes two), so it would overflow dense screens and its bold would be synthesised. Orbitron is built from geometric, capital-like shapes: its lowercase and digits (a "0" slashed like "Ø") are hard to read at UI sizes, it lacks the "·" the app uses, and it carries a Reserved Font Name. Chakra Petch is the most robotic of the readable ones, at Geist's width; Space Grotesk is the most characterful that also works as a text face. Both are offered, in the two shapes the issue described: one face throughout, or a separate display family for headings only.
- **Easier reading.** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (2025, OFL, variable 200 to 800) rather than the 2019 Atkinson Hyperlegible, which has only Regular and Bold. OpenDyslexic is the face the issue named and some readers look for by name; published studies have not found that it improves reading speed or accuracy, and its width is a real cost here, so the notes above give the owner both sides.
- **Neutral.** Inter rather than IBM Plex Sans: Plex's licence reserves the name "Plex", so a subset of our own could not keep the name, and shipping IBM's own files instead means one file per weight. Inter has no Reserved Font Name, is one variable file, and was designed for user interfaces at small sizes. The trade-off is that Inter is closer to Geist than Plex would be.
